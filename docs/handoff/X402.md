# x402 machine payments

## What it is for

An agent that exhausts its anonymous quota hits a wall it cannot pass alone:
someone has to go and sign up. x402 removes that. The agent receives a 402
describing what to pay, retries with a signed authorization, and continues. No
account, no card, no human.

This is the unbuilt half of keyless first contact.

## Wire format

x402 v2, `exact` scheme, EIP-3009 on an EVM chain:

- `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` — base64 `PaymentRequired` on our 402
- `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` — base64 `PaymentPayload` on the client's retry
- `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` — base64 `SettlementResponse` on success

`amount` is in the asset's own base units (USDC has 6 decimals, so `1000000` is
one dollar) rather than a fiat string, which keeps this server out of the
business of quoting exchange rates.

## Why we do not verify or settle locally

Settling an EIP-3009 authorization means submitting a transaction, which means a
hot wallet holding gas — an operational and custody burden this service
deliberately does not take on. Since a facilitator is required for the money to
move at all, verifying the signature ourselves would duplicate work we still
could not finish, and would mean hand-rolling secp256k1 recovery into a codebase
with nine production dependencies. The spec sanctions facilitator verification
explicitly.

The consequence is a hard gate: **without a configured facilitator, x402 is
off**, and `assertProductionSafety()` refuses to start if it is half-configured.
Advertising a price we cannot collect is worse than not accepting payment.

## The two properties that carry the risk

**Access is granted only after SETTLE, never after VERIFY.** Verify says the
signature is well-formed; settle says the money moved. Granting on verify would
hand out paid capacity for an authorization that never lands. Tested by making
verify succeed and settle fail, and asserting no credit was granted.

**An authorization is honoured at most once.** The EIP-3009 nonce is claimed by
a unique index *before* the facilitator is asked to settle — a check-then-insert
would race. This is the product's own guarantee applied to its own billing.
Tested for replay within a workspace and across workspaces.

Two smaller refusals happen before any facilitator call is spent: an
underpayment, and an authorization paying an address that is not ours. The
client chooses what it signs, so neither can be taken on trust.

A failed attempt keeps its nonce claimed rather than releasing it, so an
authorization cannot be retried into a second settlement while the first may
still be in flight somewhere.

## What is not stored

No signature and no authorization payload. The nonce is kept to deduplicate and
the settlement reference to reconcile — nothing that could be replayed if the
table leaked.

## Not done

- **Never exercised against a real facilitator.** Every test stubs it. The
  ordering and the refusals are proven; interoperability with a live facilitator
  is not.
- **No refunds.** A settled payment that we then fail to credit has no automatic
  path back.
- Only the `exact` scheme on EVM. No Solana, no `upto` scheme.
